================================================================================
QUALITATIVE EVALUATION PROTOCOL
Criteria, parameters, and experimental design
================================================================================

Version: 1.0
Language: English
Nature: Methodological document supporting the experimental protocol
Scope: To describe the design principles, the selected systems, the comparison
        pairs, the evaluation criteria, and the rationale for the
        methodological choices adopted in the qualitative evaluation of the
        study.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DESIGN PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The qualitative evaluation was designed according to five principles that
ensure validity, comparability, and feasibility of the protocol:

  1.1 Blind comparison
      The evaluator does not know which architecture came from which
      configuration. This blinding prevents expectations about the pipeline
      from influencing the judgement.

  1.2 Pairing by system
      Each system is evaluated with two architectures generated by different
      configurations, allowing controlled comparison within the same domain.

  1.3 Complexity control
      Three systems were selected to cover the complexity spectrum: one
      simple, one medium, and one complex.

  1.4 Requirements available
      The evaluator receives the system's user stories. Without them, it is
      not possible to judge functional coverage or identify missing services.

  1.5 Observable criteria
      Instead of cohesion and coupling — technical concepts that generate
      resistance and ambiguity — the adopted criteria are facts directly
      verifiable in the presented architecture.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. SELECTED SYSTEMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The selection covers three increasing levels of complexity:

  Complexity       System
  ---------------  -----------
  Simple           Jokul
  Medium           AcmeAir
  Complex          TNTConcept

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. COMPARISON PAIRS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For each system, the evaluator sees two anonymized final architectures,
labeled "Architecture 1" and "Architecture 2". The presentation order is
randomized per evaluator, so that position does not introduce bias.

  System        Architecture 1   Architecture 2
  -----------   --------------   --------------
  Jokul         C0               C1
  AcmeAir       C1               C2
  TNTConcept    C1               C3

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. RATIONALE FOR THE CHOSEN PAIRS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The set of pairs was chosen to cover, at three increasing levels of
complexity, the three contrasts that structure the ablation study:

  - In the simplest system (Jokul), the single agent (C0) and the complete
    pipeline (C1) are compared. This allows observing the global effect of
    multi-agent orchestration when dependencies are explicit and the
    architectural space is small.

  - In the medium-complexity system (AcmeAir), the complete pipeline (C1) and
    its variant without refinement (C2) are compared. This isolates the
    contribution of the Refiner in a scenario with a higher number of implicit
    interactions.

  - In the most complex system (TNTConcept), the complete pipeline (C1) and
    the variant without the Communication Specialist and without consolidation
    (C3) are compared. This exposes the combined effect of removing those two
    stages in the most challenging scenario of the corpus.

This configuration covers the three central contrast pairs of the quantitative
experiment — C0 vs C1, C1 vs C2, and C1 vs C3 — distributed across distinct
complexities, allowing the qualitative evaluation to dialogue directly with
the quantitative findings of each contrast, instead of concentrating all
comparisons on a single system profile.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. EVALUATION CRITERIA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evaluators assign a score to each architecture according to the scale below:

  1 — Very poor
  2 — Poor
  3 — Fair
  4 — Good
  5 — Very good
  Cannot evaluate this with the available information

The NA option is recorded whenever the evaluator judges that the user stories
and the architecture statement do not provide sufficient evidence to issue a
grounded judgement on the aspect being evaluated. It should not be used as a
substitute for a low score nor as a generic abstention: its use requires a
textual justification from the evaluator, indicating what was missing to
allow the evaluation.

The criteria are facts verifiable in the architecture, not abstract technical
concepts. This reduces evaluator resistance and increases consistency across
evaluations.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. EVALUATION SCOPE: GEMINI ONLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The qualitative evaluation was conducted only on the architectures generated
by Gemini. The rationale is threefold:

  6.1 Role of Gemini in the protocol
      Gemini was the model configured as primary in the experimental protocol
      and used throughout the ablation analysis. It is therefore the model
      whose architectures structure the central question of the study.

  6.2 Role of DeepSeek
      DeepSeek entered as a partial replication, with the specific goal of
      testing whether the observed patterns held under an alternative model,
      not of generating a second complete set of architectures for human
      evaluation. Including DeepSeek in the qualitative evaluation would
      double the number of architectures per evaluator (from 6 to 12) without
      bringing proportional gain: the model effect has already been discussed
      quantitatively, where the conclusions were consistent between the two
      LLMs.

  6.3 Instability of DeepSeek in consolidation
      DeepSeek showed recurrent instability in the consolidation stage, with
      frequent fallback to Proposal A. This reduces the real diversity among
      the final architectures and would weaken the premise of comparing
      distinct configurations.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. ROLE OF CLAUDE AND RATIONALE FOR ITS EXCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Claude Sonnet 4.5 participated in the quantitative study as the third model,
but was not included in the qualitative evaluation. Three reasons support this
decision.

  7.1 Absence of a role in the ablation protocol
      Claude was neither the primary model nor the designated partial
      replication. Its inclusion in the quantitative study aimed to broaden
      the coverage of commercial models and verify whether the F1 patterns
      held under a third provider. It does not participate in the central
      question about orchestration mechanisms, which is answered by the
      ablation analysis conducted on Gemini.

  7.2 Irregular quality of the emitted specifications
      Claude showed the highest rate of malformed specifications among the
      three models, both before and after the normalization employed in the
      evaluation. Since the qualitative evaluation requires the evaluator to
      read and judge the final architecture, artifacts with compromised
      structure would introduce noise into the judgement and could penalize
      the model for a serialization failure rather than for a poor
      architectural decision. This would contaminate the premise of comparing
      distinct configurations under the same conditions.

  7.3 Evaluator cost versus proportional gain
      Including Claude would triple the number of architectures per evaluator
      (from 6 to 18) without answering a new research question. The model
      effect has already been discussed quantitatively, where the conclusions
      on the central contrasts were consistent between Gemini and DeepSeek.
      Keeping the qualitative evaluation restricted to Gemini preserves
      blinding and avoids turning the qualitative analysis into a secondary
      comparison between models.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. RATIONALE FOR THE EXCLUSION OF C4
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The C4 configuration — in which the Communication Specialist is replaced by a
generalist architect (Agent 2.1) — was not included in the qualitative
evaluation. The decision rests on three arguments.

  8.1 C4 is not an ablation, it is a substitution
      The ablation study investigates what each stage of the pipeline
      contributes to architecture quality. C0, C1, C2, and C3 remove or
      preserve stages; C4 swaps one stage for another of a different nature.
      Since the qualitative evaluation was designed to dialogue with the
      three ablation contrasts (C0 vs C1, C1 vs C2, and C1 vs C3), including
      C4 would require a distinct comparison question, which is not the same
      question that guides the qualitative protocol.

  8.2 Partial overlap with C1
      C4 maintains the general structure of the complete pipeline (two
      proposals, consolidation, and refinement) and differs from C1
      essentially in the prompt weight of the second agent. From the human
      evaluator's point of view, C4 and C1 tend to produce architectures with
      similar service structure, which would reduce the useful diversity
      among the presented pairs and weaken the premise of comparing distinct
      configurations.

  8.3 Focus on the central question
      The qualitative evaluation exists to provide interpretive material for
      the quantitative findings of the three main contrasts. Adding C4 would
      shift the evaluator's focus to a comparison between two variants of the
      complete pipeline, instead of keeping attention on the orchestration
      mechanisms that the ablation study actually tests.

Keeping the qualitative evaluation restricted to Gemini preserves blinding,
keeps the evaluator's load at a feasible level, and keeps the study's focus on
the central question — which orchestration mechanisms matter — instead of
turning the qualitative analysis into a secondary comparison between models or
between variants of the complete pipeline.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. DESIGN SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Dimension                 Choice
  ------------------------  -----------------------------------------------
  Model evaluated           Gemini
  Systems                   3 (Jokul, AcmeAir, TNTConcept)
  Complexities              Simple, medium, complex
  Pairs per system          2 anonymized architectures
  Contrasts covered         C0 vs C1, C1 vs C2, C1 vs C3
  Presentation order        Randomized per evaluator
  Evaluator input           System user stories
  Scale                     1 to 5, plus justified NA
  Criteria                  Facts verifiable in the architecture
  Excluded models           DeepSeek, Claude
  Excluded configuration    C4
